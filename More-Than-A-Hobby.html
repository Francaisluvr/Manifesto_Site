More Than a Hobby: Being Seen as a Creator, Not Just a Consumer

Scrolling. Pinning. Watching tutorials. Liking. Saving. Repeat. Sound familiar? It’s easy to feel like your creativity only exists in the digital ether, a spectator in a world of makers. But here’s the thing: you are more than a hobbyist. You’re a creator. And it’s time the world sees you that way.

Identity as a Maker

For too long, words like “crafty” have been tossed around like cute little labels—sometimes endearing, sometimes dismissive. But let’s reclaim it. To be crafty is to make things with intention, skill, and care. It’s a badge of honor, not a side note.

When you pick up your scissors, thread, or sketchbook, you aren’t just “doing a hobby.” You are shaping ideas into reality. You’re showing off that part of you that makes you unique. That tote bag, that embroidered patch, that tiny sketch—they’re all proof that you are a creator.

Why the World Needs Your Creativity

You might think a small project is “too minor” to matter. I beg to differ. Every stitch, every brushstroke, every handmade item has the power to connect, inspire, and delight. The world needs your creativity just as much as you do—not because it has to be sold or shared, but because it carries a piece of you that no one else can replicate.

Your tiny tote might brighten someone’s day. Your hand-dyed fabric might spark another maker’s imagination. Your tiny sketch might help someone breathe through a rough day. Your courage to try and fail might give someone else permission to pick up their own scissors.

Navigating Dismissive Comments

Ah, the classic: “That’s cute!” versus the magical, validating, “You made that?!”

The first can feel like a shrug, a subtle dismissal. The second? It’s recognition. It’s acknowledgment that your hands, your ideas, and your effort created something tangible.

Learning to accept compliments, to own your skills, and to say with pride, “Yes, I made that” is part of claiming your identity as a creator. Because making is not just about objects—it’s about presence, intention, and showing up as yourself.

Finding Your Creative Voice

How do you make sure every project carries you?

    *
  • Infuse personal touches—colors, patterns, or techniques that make your work unmistakably yours.
    • *
  • Experiment freely—push boundaries, embrace happy accidents, let mistakes guide you.
    • *
  • Reflect on your why—ask yourself, “Why do I love this?” The answer will guide every stitch, brushstroke, or idea.
    • *
  • Share selectively—choose spaces and people who celebrate your work, not just your aesthetic.
Your creative voice grows with every project, every “oops,” and every finish line crossed. And trust me—it’s worth it.
